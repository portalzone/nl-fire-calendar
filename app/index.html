The challenge, made local

Two sensors count fire hotspots differently. This app makes their history comparable.

MODIS provides the long record, while VIIRS detects smaller hot areas. We count daily fire cells and use the years when both sensors operated to adjust earlier MODIS months to the VIIRS level.

This Newfoundland and Labrador prototype is built around the challenge's intended users: emergency responders, scientists and land managers who need historical patterns, unusual months and critical periods for a selected area.

How to read this

  • Each square in the calendar is one month. Dark purple means little burning, bright yellow means a lot, and grey means none was detected.
  • The number counts fire cells: map squares of 0.1° (about 11 km north to south and 7 km east to west here) with at least one satellite heat detection on a given day. One square on one day counts once, however many detections it has. The older MODIS sensor and the newer, more sensitive VIIRS sensors are put on one scale, so you can compare 2005 with 2025.
  • The median is the middle value for the same month in other years. It is our multi-year baseline. It is less thrown off by one extreme year than an average is.
  • A heat detection is not a confirmed fire. Industrial sites, gas flares and other hot surfaces can also be detected, although the known ones are removed.

1. Choose an area and dates

With a mouse, drag a rectangle on the map. On a touch screen, turn on "Draw area on map by touch" first. Fire cells counts each 0.1° map cell with a detection on a given day once, so the finer VIIRS pixels do not inflate the count. Raw hotspots count every detection.
